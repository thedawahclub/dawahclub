BRIEF INTRODUCTION TO CHRISTIANITY
The following article is written primarily from a Christian perspective so that a Muslim daa'ee can better understand what Christians believe. The goal is to present the material in a way that is unbiased, accurate, informative and helpful during respectful dawah discussions.

Christianity is a monotheistic religion based on the life and teachings of Jesus of Nazareth, who lived in Palestine during the first century. The primary source of Christian belief is the Bible.

Christianity is the world's largest religion, with approximately two billion followers worldwide. Its followers are called Christians, meaning followers of Christ.

Jesus of Nazareth (approximately 0–33 CE), a Jew from Palestine, began a movement within Judaism that eventually came to be known as Christianity after his death.

According to the Bible, Jesus was a practicing Jew who challenged many of the religious practices and traditions of the Jewish religious establishment. Although Christians believe that Jesus fulfilled the Scriptures, he was rejected by many of the religious authorities of his time.

His followers considered him to be the Jewish Messiah as prophesied in the Scriptures. The Jewish religious establishment, however, regarded him as a heretic and a rebel and eventually handed him over to the Romans for persecution. According to the Bible, Jesus was tried and crucified by the Romans around the age of 33. Christians believe that he was buried, resurrected in a glorified physical body on the third day, and ascended into heaven after forty days.

The Christian Church officially began ten days after the ascension of Jesus when the Holy Spirit descended upon the disciples and other followers on the Feast of Pentecost (fifty days after Jesus' resurrection). Christians believe this event enabled the disciples to speak in tongues (previously unknown languages). On that occasion Peter, one of the apostles, stood before believers and non-believers and delivered what is regarded as the first Christian sermon.

The disciples then began missionary campaigns. They first preached among the Jews and later expanded their efforts to the Gentiles, inviting them to embrace Christianity.

The conversion of Paul around 35 CE was a major turning point in early Christianity. Paul was a highly educated Greek-speaking Jew who became one of Christianity's greatest missionaries. He traveled extensively throughout the Roman Empire preaching to Gentiles, establishing churches, and writing letters that explained and defended the Christian faith.

Paul strongly argued that Gentile converts should not be required to observe circumcision, dietary laws, and many of the Mosaic laws. These positions were not universally accepted among the earliest Jewish Christians but eventually became foundational to mainstream Christianity.

Paul's teachings and writings shaped Christianity as it is understood today. More than half of the books of the New Testament are traditionally attributed to him.

Roman Emperor Constantine's conversion to Christianity in 312 CE marked another major turning point. Prior to this, Christians had often been a persecuted minority within the Roman Empire. After Constantine embraced Christianity, the religion spread rapidly throughout the empire.

In 325 CE, Emperor Constantine convened the First Council of Nicaea. This council established the Nicene Creed and sought to define a unified Christian doctrine for the church. The council affirmed the mainstream Trinitarian understanding of Christianity, which became the dominant position throughout Christendom.

From this point onward Christianity continued to grow and became firmly established throughout much of Europe and beyond.

CORE BELIEFS OF CHRISTIANITY
Christians believe that sin entered humanity through the disobedience of Adam and Eve. Sin separates mankind from God, and to restore this broken relationship God sent His only Son, Jesus Christ.

Christians believe that Jesus lived on earth as both fully God and fully man. They believe that he died on the cross for the sins of humanity, was buried, rose again from the dead, ascended into heaven, and now sits at the right hand of God the Father making intercession for believers.

According to mainstream Christian belief, Jesus' death on the cross completely paid the penalty for the sins of humanity, making salvation available to all who believe in him.

The core beliefs of Christianity can be summarized as follows:

• Jesus is the Christ (Messiah).
• Jesus is the Son of God.
• Jesus is fully divine and fully human.
• Jesus died for the sins of humanity.
• Jesus was buried.
• Jesus was resurrected.
• Those who believe in him can receive salvation.

Christianity today is generally divided into three major branches:

(1) Roman Catholicism
(2) Eastern Orthodoxy
(3) Protestantism

Roman Catholicism is the largest branch, with approximately 1.2 billion adherents.

The Catholic Church divided into the Roman Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church during the Great Schism of 1054 CE.

The Protestant Reformation of the sixteenth century resulted in the formation of Protestant Christianity.

In addition to these major branches, Christianity also includes many independent churches, denominations, and movements throughout the world.

ESSENTIAL BELIEFS OF CHRISTIANITY
Throughout its two thousand year history Christianity has developed many branches, denominations, sects, and movements. While differences exist, the following beliefs are generally regarded as central by the majority of Christians.

Monotheism and the Trinity

Christians believe in one God who exists eternally as three divine Persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

According to Trinitarian belief:

• There is exactly one God.
• The Father is God.
• The Son is God.
• The Holy Spirit is God.
• The Father is not the Son.
• The Son is not the Holy Spirit.
• The Father is not the Holy Spirit.
• Each Person is fully God while remaining distinct from the others.

Although the doctrine of the Trinity is not expressed in one single verse, Christians believe it is taught throughout Scripture and regard it as the central mystery of the Christian faith.

The Savior and Deity of Christ

Christians believe that Jesus Christ is the only begotten Son of God the Father, not made nor created, but eternally begotten. They believe Jesus is God, existed before His incarnation, and is co-eternal and co-equal with the Father.

Christians believe that Jesus was sent by the Father into the world to become man and save humanity from its sins. They believe He was conceived by the Virgin Mary through the power of the Holy Spirit. Therefore, Jesus had God as His Father and Mary as His human mother. Christians teach that Jesus possesses two natures—fully God and fully man.

According to Christian belief, the primary reason Jesus became man was to redeem humanity from original sin. Christians believe that His suffering and crucifixion paid the ransom for mankind's sins and satisfied God's justice, making salvation available.

Christians believe Jesus died, rose bodily from the dead in a glorified and immortal body, ascended into heaven, and now sits at the right hand of the Father where He continually intercedes for believers. They also believe He will return in glory to judge both the living and the dead.

SALVATION
According to Christianity, salvation is only possible through Jesus Christ. Christians believe that all people are sinners who are separated from God and deserving of eternal punishment because of sin.

Christians believe that Jesus' death on the cross paid the penalty for the sins of mankind, making forgiveness and eternal life possible.

Many Protestants believe salvation comes through faith alone in Jesus Christ. Roman Catholics and Eastern Orthodox Christians generally teach that salvation involves faith in Jesus Christ together with a life of obedience, good works, and participation in the life of the Church.

THE SECOND COMING OF JESUS
All mainstream Christians believe that Jesus Christ will return again at the end of time.

This future event is known as the Second Coming. Christians believe Jesus will establish the Kingdom of God in its fullness, judge humanity, destroy evil, reward the righteous, and bring everlasting peace.

DEATH AND THE AFTERLIFE
Most Christians believe that every person will face divine judgment after death.

They believe those who have accepted God's salvation will receive eternal life, while those who reject it will face eternal punishment.

Roman Catholics, Eastern Orthodox Christians, and many Protestants also believe that every individual experiences a personal judgment immediately after death before the final resurrection and Last Judgment.

THE HOLY BIBLE
The Holy Bible is the primary sacred scripture of Christianity.

The word Bible comes from the Greek word biblia, meaning "books."

Christians generally believe that approximately forty human authors, from many different backgrounds and occupations, contributed to the writing of the Bible over a period of about 1,500 years (approximately 1450 BC to AD 150).

Although written by many human authors, Christians believe that God is the ultimate Author and that the writers recorded exactly what God intended through divine inspiration.

For most Christians, the Bible is the final authority in matters of faith and practice.

The Bible is divided into two major sections:

• The Old Testament
• The New Testament

The Old Testament corresponds largely to the Hebrew Scriptures and was written primarily in Hebrew, with small portions in Aramaic.

The New Testament consists of twenty-seven books written in Greek during the first century AD.

The Roman Catholic Bible contains seventy-three books.

The Protestant Bible contains sixty-six books.

Both traditions include the same twenty-seven books in the New Testament. The difference is found in the Old Testament, where the Catholic Bible contains forty-six books and the Protestant Bible contains thirty-nine books.

BRANCHES OF CHRISTIANITY
Christianity has developed into many branches, denominations, sects, and movements because of differences in theology, doctrine, church government, traditions, and religious practices.

A branch (or denomination) is generally regarded as part of mainstream Christianity if it accepts the core Christian beliefs, including the divinity of Jesus Christ, the Trinity, and the authority of the Bible.

Relationships between different branches vary from close cooperation to disagreement over doctrine.

The major branches of Christianity include:

(1) Roman Catholic
(2) Eastern Orthodox
(3) Oriental Orthodox
(4) Anglican / Episcopal
(5) Protestant

Major Protestant denominations include:

(i) Lutheran
(ii) Anabaptists (Amish, Hutterites, Mennonites)
(iii) Baptist
(iv) Quakers
(v) Methodist
(vi) Reformed Churches (Calvinist and Presbyterian)
(vii) Pentecostal (Assemblies of God, Apostolic Church, New Apostolic Church)
(viii) Seventh-day Adventist (considered by some to be a Protestant denomination and by others to be outside mainstream Protestantism)

CULTS CLAIMING TO BE CHRISTIAN
A cult of Christianity is generally understood as a group that claims to be Christian while rejecting one or more central teachings historically accepted by mainstream Christianity.

In general, the majority of Christians do not regard these groups as belonging to orthodox Christianity.

Some well-known examples include:

(1) Jehovah's Witnesses
(2) The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Mormons)
