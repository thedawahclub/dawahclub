****** WHAT IS WITHIN ISLAM AND OUTSIDE OF (SECTS, CULTS) ISLAM ******

Suppose you are one of those individuals who searched and found Islam and became Muslim and it happened that you encountered individuals, books or websites that call themselves Muslims, but there are certain beliefs and practices that seem unusual. Or, you may have been to introduced to Islam by someone or a source that seem to be at odds with the majority of the Muslims. Or, You simply came across few groups and not sure who to follow.

KEEP IN MIND THE FOLLOWING THREE POINTS: (1) Islam is a complete religion and division in Islam is forbidden. (2) What is within Islam? Legitimate differences of opinions which is are allowed and not considered as division. (3) What is outside of Islam? The deviant sects and cults are not part of Islam.

ISLAM IS A COMPLETE RELIGION & DIVISION IN ISLAM IS FORBIDDEN
**************************************************************************************
The very first thing you should understand is that Islam is complete and Allah commands Unity . Allah directly (in Quran) and indirectly (through Prophet Muhammad-saws) forbade and warned us against division. Allah said many places in Quran:
(i) "This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion." (Quran 5:3)
(ii) And hold fast, all of you together, to the Rope of Allah (i.e. this Quran), and be not divided among yourselves.." (Quran 3:103)
(iii) Indeed, those who have divided their religion and become sects - you (Muhammad) are not [associated] with them in anything. (Quran 6:159)



WHAT IS WITHIN ISLAM:
******************************
Following Allah's Order and Guidance is considered within Islam. These information is found in Quran and in authentic Hadith collections.

The Prophet (saws) lived according to Allah's Guidance and set an example for us to follow. The way he lived, called Sunnah (that is his saying, actions, approvals, disapprovals, etc.) is recorded in authentic Hadith collections. Let us know the following terms before further discussions.
* 'Shariah' means total collection of the Islamic laws which are found in Quran and Hadith.
* 'Fiqh' is a collection of laws deduced from Quran and Hadith to cover specific situations not directly covered in Shariah.
* 'Ijtihad' is the process of finding solutions to new circumstances through interpretation of Quran and Hadith. 'Mujtahid' is a scholar involved in Ijtihad. When there is an agreement of all the mujtahids on a solution (a decision reached through Ijtihad) we call it 'Ijmaa' (scholarly consensus).
* 'Qiyas' (deriving rules through analogy from Quran and Hadith) a specific variant of the general concept of Ijtihad.

### During the Prophet's lifetime, the only source of Islamic law was divine revelation either direct (which is found in Quran) or indirect through the Prophet (which is found in Hadith). The Prophet (saws) also taught his companions (Sahabah) how to find solutions to problems (through Ijtihad) using Quran and his Sunnah.

### After the death (around 632 CE) of the Prophet (saws), new situations arose (which were not directly dealt in Quran or Hadith) as Islam was spreading to outside of Arabia during the time of the four Righteous Caliphs and the major Sahabahs. In that era (the first 30 years after the prophet's death) they used Ijtihad and Ijmaa extensively to derive solutions to new problems and thus set example for the later generations to follow.

In that era, the combined approval of the Righteous Caliphs and the Sahabahs in the matter of legal rulings promoted unity and provided little or no opportunity for factionalism among the Muslims. As far as Fiqh was concerned, only one Madhhab (school of Islamic legal thought) existed during the period of the Righteous Caliphs. Although some Sahabah debated and differed on some points and certain interpretations , their differences rarely reached the level of disunity and factionalism.

### Thirty years after Prophet's (saws) death, as Islam spread far beyond the borders of Arabia and masses of people from all sorts of background (language, culture, religion) were entering into Islam, more and more new situations came for Islamic interpretation with fewer scholarly sahabahs available (in faraway lands) for interpretation and judgment.
* By then, most major Muslim Provinces (countries as we call now) had Copies of the Quran and had few scholarly sahabahs (as most of the scholarly Sahabah stayed in Arabia). During that time Sunnah of the Prophet was not written and gathered in volumes as he have today in the Hadith collections. The scholarly sahabahs memorized many narrations from the Prophet (saws), and some had personal written materials ( a practice they started during the later part of the Prophet's life).
* But not every scholarly sahabah knew every Sunnah or interpretation of every verse of the Quran from the Prophet. So, at times on new situations and problems sahabahs (who lived in faraway provinces) acted and made judgment based on what they knew and their training from the Prophet (saws).
* They were always careful to relate that the opinion and judgment was their own (based on Quran and Sunnah) and they were not free from error as they pointed out the saying of the Prophet, "If a judge gives a verdict according to the best of his knowledge (Ijtihad) and his verdict is correct (i.e. agrees with Allah and His Prophet's verdict) he will receive a double reward, and if he gives a verdict according to the best of his knowledge and his verdict is wrong, even then he will get a single reward ." (Bukhari 7352).
* And as we will find out later, that these judgments made in different parts of the Muslim world were not always identical.
**So, we can see the early beginning of slightly different Fiqh ruling (on new issues) in different parts of the Muslim world. And their students (Tabi'een) on those areas learned and taught the next generation of students ( Taba -at-Tabi'een).

### About one Hundred years after Prophet's (saws) death, we see the beginning of a formal school of Islamic legal thought (known as Madhhab) in Iraq. Later on, this school would be known as "Hanafi Madhhab" by the name of its most knowledgeable scholar and jurist Imam Abu Hanifa (699-767 CE).
** Around the same time in Madinah we see another well known scholar Imam Malik (711-795 CE) becoming famous for his Jurisprudence and his school would come to be known as "Maliki Madhhab".
** Few years later we would see another well known scholar and jurist from Makkah by the name Imam Ash-Shafee (767-820 CE) and his school would come to be known as "Shafee Madhhab".
** And few years later, we see the last of the four major schools "Hanbali Madhhab" named after the famous scholar and jurist Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal (780-855 CE).
**All these four famous scholars and jurists were from the generation of Tabi'een or Taba-at-Tabi'een, of whom the Prophet (saws) said, "The best people are those of my generation, and then those who will come after them (i.e. the Tabi'een ), and then those who will come after them (i.e. the Taba -at-Tabi'een)…" (Bukhari 6429).
Due to the circumstances mentioned above not all their legal decisions on certain new issues and problems were identical. This is why we see slightly different rulings and practices among the Muslims today.
***The four Madhhabs were founded in different parts of the Muslim world before all major Hadith compilations were made.

### Within 250 years of Prophet's (saws) death, we had one of earliest extensive collection of Hadith (Musnad Ahmad by Imam Ahmed ibn Hanbal) and the two most authentic collections of Hadith (Bukhari and Muslim).
* After the death of the Prophet (saws) the leading Sahabah realized the pressing need of preserving all his sayings and actions as he was no longer there for consultation. The scholarly sahabahs wrote down and taught others (Tabi'een) what they knew and learned from the Prophet.
* With the spread of Islam narration of hadiths became widespread. Some people (though few in numbers) would narrate hadith with faulty memory, without proper reference (chain of narration), their personal opinion or some even fabricated Hadith.
* To combat this situation we see during the era of the Tabi'een, scholars specializing and devoting their life in compilation of hadiths. We also see scholars focusing on Hadith instead of the existing traditional rulings of the earlier scholars to resolve the problems of Fiqh.
* Great scholars of Hadith like Imam Bukhari (810-870 CE) and Imam Muslim (817-875 CE) dedicated their life to carefully collect authentic Hadith of the Prophet (saws) from all possible sources. This process lead us to have all major Hadith compilations (mainly the famous six Hadith collections) within 270 years after Prophet's (saws) death.

### From 250 years after Prophet's death till modern time. As time went on (with the invention of paper and printing) the entire collections of Hadith were becoming more easily available to the scholars of all parts of the world. Some early scholars noticed gaps and discrepancies in Hadith collections other than Bukhari and Muslim. Some of them dedicated their lives to authenticate the vast number of hadiths available in different collections.

Some later scholars (and their followers) of many schools (Madhhabs) changed their positions on existing early rulings based on proof from the available authentic Hadith collections. While others simply chose to continue to follow the existing rulings of the past.

### IMPORTANT POINTS TO KEEP IN MIND:
(1) Muslims consider legitimate differences in opinion between the four Madhhabs (schools of Islamic legal thought) within the boundaries of Islam and not as division.
(2) The differences among the Madhhabs are few and trivial in nature. These differences do not affect any fundamental beliefs, pillars and major practices of Islam.
(3) Primarily due to Political reasons and preference of early ruling dynasties (for example, Ottoman empires codified their Islamic laws according to Hanafi Madhhab) we see certain parts of Muslim world following rulings of a certain Madhhab.
(4) One is free to follow a scholar of a particular Madhhab. For a person with little or no knowledge of Islam (layman), he is not allowed to derive rules from Quran and Hadith. An individual must learn (directly or indirectly) from knowledgeable, trustworthy and pious scholar (or sources). Scholars with the above qualities usually give references from Quran and Hadith for their particular opinion. Selectively choosing rules (for personal gain without valid reason) from different Madhhabs is also considered inappropriate.
(5) Finally, a person may follow Hanafi Madhhab (or any other Madhhab) but if he finds a particular opinion of a scholar to be stronger (in terms of references from Quran and Hadith) than that of the Hanafi Madhhab, and follows that opinion than that is more appropriate.

WHAT IS OUTSIDE OF ISLAM: (DEVIANT SECTS AND CULTS)
**************************************************************************
The sects and cults frequently use the term Islam or Muslim causing confusions among the new Muslims (converts). The sects may have some similarities and harder to identify. The Cults have major differences and easier to identify.
Our goal is not to offend anyone but to understand what we as Muslims consider unacceptable according to the Guidance of Allah.
It is totally unacceptable in Islam to offend any individual for their beliefs and practices. Our job is to sincerely advice them of the real truth and convey the true message of Allah.

Following is list of qualities of deviant sects and cults;
* (i) Disregard for proofs and evidences from Quran and authentic Hadith collections.
* (ii) Only accepting Quran and rejecting the Hadith (Sunnah of the Prophet Muhammad-saws) claiming those were not preserved or trustworthy.
* (iii) Rejecting and disrespecting the four righteous Caliphs and the rest of the Sahabah (companions) of the Prophet Muhammad (saws).
* (iv) Disregarding and disrespecting the mothers of the believers (the wives of the Prophet-saws).
* (v) Accepting a living person (a leader, Imam, scholar, etc.) or a dead person (by visiting the grave) as mediator between Allah and themselves, and asking for help and intercession.
* (vi) Accepting a person dead or alive after Prophet Muhammad (saws) as a Prophet ( or Messenger) or giving similar status (of love, respect and obedience) using other terms.
* (vii) Accepting new sources of religious law (other than Quran and Hadith) whether by direct revelation, dream or own intellect of a person (usually the cult founder).
* (viii) Blindly following a person (usually the sect or cult leader or founder) and his teachings without verification from Quran and authentic Hadith.

### SECTS: In general sects originate out of Islam and eventually deviate so much away from the true teachings of Islam that is it hard to believe they are within the fold of Islam. Example: Shia.

** SHIA (Shiaism): The followers are known as Shias or Shiites. Today they are about one tenth (7 to 10%) of the total Muslim population and primarily concentrated in Iran.
* This group (with few individuals) started with a political dispute over who is to succeed as the leader (the head of state) of the Muslims after the death of Prophet Muhammad (saws). They rejected the first three righteous Caliphs (head of state or leader of the Muslims) and regarded Ali, the fourth Caliph, as Prophet Muhammad's (saws) first true successor.
* In the early stages, what started as a movement known "Shiat Ali" or the "Party of Ali" would eventually reject almost all the Sahabahs (Companions of the Prophet-saws) and some of the wives of the Prophet (saws); and utter lies and curse towards them. In the process (after the death of Ali, Hasan and Hussain) they would go on to add beliefs and practices that have nothing to do with Islam or the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad (saws).
* In their rejection of almost all the Sahabah (thus almost all the authentic teachings of the Prophet-saws) they would deviate so far away from Islam that their version of Islam is no longer resembled what the Prophet Muhammad (saws) preached and established by the order of Allah.
* This group established something called the Imamate (political and spiritual leadership) of 12 Imams starting with Ali, Hasan, Hussain and nine more from Hussain's lineage. They believe the twelfth (also the Mahdi) Imam, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-‘Askari (born around 969 CE), is still alive and living in the Tunnel of Samarra (in Iran) hidden from rest of us. He appears only to those who are most in need and to those who are most virtuous and pious.
* They exaggerate about their Imams, giving them status and qualities equal ( or more in some qualities compared to all other prophets except prophet Muhammad-saws) to the prophets, claiming that they are appointed by God, infallible, sinless, perfect in highest sense of the word, have knowledge of the unseen, etc. They believe Imams have direct knowledge from God, and their verdict is the verdict of God. They devote many acts of worship to or through (mediator) them such as supplication, seeking help, offering sacrifices and tawaf (circumambulating their tombs), etc.
* They claim that many of the verses of the Quran's meaning is only known to the Imams and only they can interpret the Quran. They also have their own version of Hadith most of which are fabricated or attributed to their Imams.
They believe in Taqiyah (or Taqiya meaning conceal or hide), which means they are allowed to hide or lie about their religion or belief (or practices) when they feel necessary, usually to save life or perceiving injustice, unequal treatment or to save honor and property.
* Their mourning ceremonies such as Ashura, in which they flagellate themselves to commemorate Hussein’s death at Karbala, has nothing to do with Islam.
* In terms of practices their Sahadah, Adhan, prayer , etc., has major or minor differences from the mainstream Muslims. They also permit Muttah (fixed-term temporary marriage) which is forbidden in Islam.

### CULTS: Cults may or may not originate out of Islam but are clearly outside Islam even though they may use the word Islam, Muslim, Quran or certain practices of Islam. Some examples of cults are listed below.

** Ahmadiyya (Qadiyani, Qadiani or Ahmadi ): This cult is officially known as Ahmadiyya Muslim Community (or Ahmadiyya Muslim Jama'at).This cult is founded in 1889 by Mirza Ghulam Ahmad (1835-1908) of Qadian, Punjab, India. He claimed himself to be the long awaited Messiah of the old testament , a new Prophet and the second coming of Jesus. This cult is very difficult to detect for a new Muslim as they identify themselves as Muslims and Islam as their religion, and hold to many of the outwardly practices of Islam.**

** Ismaili (Aga Khani or Nizari): The Shia Imami Ismaili Muslims, generally known as the Ismailis, is a cult split out of Shia on disagreement over their choice of the seventh Imam. The dominant Ismaili branch believe in the line of Imamat in hereditary succession continuing from Ali to seventh Imam Ismail to His Highness the Aga Khan, who is their present, 49th Imam. This cult is easier to identify as its beliefs and practices are very different from Islam.**

** Nation of Islam: The Nation of Islam was founded by Wallace Muhammad in Detroit in 1930. This cult has nothing to do with Islam except that it uses the word Islam on its name.**
