### Two important points about our Shariah (Islamic law) we must clearly understand as new Muslims.
**(1) When it comes to strictly "religious matters" (articles of belief and religious rituals/actions) such as the issues we dealt in articles 2 through 10, are governed by the principle of restriction, which means everything is impermissible (forbidden) unless it is proven that it is prescribed in our Shariah (meaning prescribed by Allah). That means no act (or religious belief) of worship may be performed without textual evidence from the Quran and the authentic Hadith collections.
**(2) When it comes to our all others actions (general actions) of daily life, things that we all humans do, such as dressing, eating, working, learning, sleeping, behaving, business dealings, etc., are all permissible unless there is evidence from the Shariah which restricts it. Which means nothing is considered forbidden unless our Shariah forbids it.

Article # 2: PURPOSE OF LIFE IN ISLAM (AIM OF OUR LIFE)
**The most important question in life is " what is the purpose of life?"

### As Muslims the answer is clear and simple. Allah tells us in Quran that He created us to worship Him.
"And I (Allah) did not create the jinn and humankind except to worship Me'" (Quran 51:56)

**That is it! The sole purpose of being on this earth is to only worship Allah. There is no ambiguity in this or in anything else in Islam.

**But how can we be in worship of Allah 24/7 (all the time). We are human, we need to eat, work, sleep and do other things to function. Well, the Islamic understanding of worship, allows all actions of our life to be acts of worship.

*If we follow the Guidance of Allah and live our life accordingly than even the most enjoyable actions (such as the intimate relation with one's spouse) of our life will be considered as acts of worship and will be rewarded accordingly in the Hereafter. This is perfection, the highest level (in terms of piety) a Muslim can reach or to the very least aim to reach.

*So, all actions (our daily mundane stuff) of our life have the potential to be acts of worship if those are done (1) sincerely for the sake of Allah (that is to please Him) and (2) according to Allah's Guidance. If a Muslim fulfills the above two conditions in every aspect of his life, he is deemed to be worshiping Allah all the time.

*For example, when a Muslim sleeps fulfilling the above two conditions, his sleep is considered as an act of worship. When he wakes up and cleans (using bathroom) himself for the preparation of early morning prayer, if he does those fulfilling the two conditions, the entire session is counted as act of worship. Then the actual ritual worship of morning prayer (Fajr Salah) must be done fulfilling the 2 conditions. Then if he goes out to do some morning exercise to keep his body (which is a gift and trust from Allah) healthy and eats his breakfast fulfilling the two conditions, then all these will be considered as acts of worship. In this way a Muslim can always be in the state of worship to Allah.

**By doing the obligatory ritual worships (the daily five prayers, yearly fasting, etc.) and staying away from the major sins, a Muslim fulfills the minimum requirement of his purpose of life.

*In our daily life there are always opportunities in everything we do to be considered as an act of worship, as Allah's messenger Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said:
"...To judge justly between two persons is regarded as Sadaqa (meaning Charity-an act of worship), and to help a man concerning his riding animal by helping him to ride it or by lifting his luggage on to it, is also regarded as Sadaqa, and (saying) a good word is also Sadaqa, and every step taken on one's way to offer the compulsory prayer (Salah in the mosque) is also Sadaqa and to remove a harmful thing from the way is also Sadaqa." (Sahih al-Bukhari 2989)

### Why do we worship Allah?
**(1) The primary and number one reason that we worship Allah because he is the only one worthy of being worshiped. Allah is worthy of worship because of Who He is. Even if He did not create us or the heavens, He would still be worthy of worship. Allah is worthy of worship because He is the Most Beautiful, Most Perfect, Most High, Most Majestic, etc (the 99 most beautiful names and attributes of Allah that we know of). When there was nothing He was worthy of Worship and when there will be nothing He would still be worthy of worship. Allah being worthy of worship is independent of who is worshiping Him.

**(2) We worship Allah because He has created us, blessed us with our lives, with our wealth, with our families and with everything else that we have.

**(3) We worship Allah because worship brings Taqwa (righteousness, awareness and fear of Allah). Taqwa brings everything else, such as stability, meaning, values, morality, etc. in our life.

**(4) We worship Allah because in the worship of Allah we will gain His pleasure. And as a reward out of His mercy Allah will grant Jannah (Paradise) to those whom He is pleased with. Allah said in Quran:
"O mankind, worship your Lord (Allah), who created you and those before you, that you may become righteous (al-Muttaqwun)" (Quran 2:21)
" Nay! But worship Allah (Alone and none else), and be among the grateful." (Quran 39:66)
" And give good tidings to those who believe and do righteous deeds that they will have gardens [in Paradise] beneath which rivers flow.." (Quran 2:25)

### How do we worship Allah?
**We worship Allah according to His guidance and instructions, which are found in Quran and in the Sunnah (the way) of His Prophet (Pbuh). And that means we have to study our religion to have knowledge of Allah's Guidance. And only with proper knowledge and sincerity (only for the sake of Allah) we can perfect our worship.
*There is no room for blindly following and repeating rituals without knowledge and sincerity in Islam. People who have knowledge are the ones who truly fear (are aware/conscious of) Allah. As Allah said in Quran:
"...Only those fear Allah, from among His servants, who have knowledge..." (Quran 35:28)

### Finally,
**Allah does not need our worship. Allah does not get any more Majestic or any more Greater through our worship. Our worship do not quench Allah's thirst or hunger or anything else we may think of. What it does is, it pleases Allah to see that we are in total submission to Him, even when we are given the choice and free-will to disbelieve and disobey Him.

**This life is a test for us from Allah to see if we are able to fulfill the purpose we were created for. To submit to Him alone and follow His Guidance to the best of our ability with proper knowledge and sincerity. Allah should be worshiped at all times, in times of hardship and in times of ease and only through this we can be successful in this life and Hereafter. Allah said in Quran:
" Who (Allah) has created death and life, that He may test you which of you is best in deed. And He is the All-Mighty, the Oft-Forgiving;" (Quran 67:2)

Article # 4: QURAN (THE PRIMARY SOURCE OF ISLAMIC INFORMATION)
*The Quran is the Last and Final Revelation from Allah.
*The Quran is a complete guide covering all aspects of our life.
*The Quran is the inimitable and unique Arabic Speech or Word of Allah revealed orally to Prophet Mohammad (pbuh) by angel Gabriel in wording and meaning, which has been preserved in memory (by memorization) and in writing and reached us by numerous authentic continuous (mutawatir) transmissions.

**BRIEF HISTORY**
************************
* The entire Quran was revealed to prophet Muhammad (pbuh) gradually over 23 years.This revelation began in one night known as the Night of Decree (Laylat Al-Qadr) in the month of Ramadan (9th month of Islamic calendar) when Prophet Muhammad was about 40 years old and ended with his death at age sixty three.
* During the Prophet's (pbuh) lifetimethe entire Quran was memorized by many companions, written down in different materials and kept in secured possession of the Prophet and other prominent companions.
* Immediately after the Prophet's (pbuh) death (632/633 CE) Abu Baker (leader of the believers) gathered all of the main scribes, huffaz (memorizer of Quran), all written materials of the Quran, and had it written down on parchment and made a single volume which was correct, authentic and complete.
* During Uthman's Caliphate, Uthman in consultation with other prominent companions of the Prophet decided to make several official copies from the single volume using the same methodology that was used by Abu Baker. Uthman then sent these official copies along with an expert companion to each Muslim province and ordered to destroy all other unofficial, partial and personal Quranic materials.
* Quran we use today in all over the world are made from those copies. Unlike other scriptures that were changed with human interpolations and omissions, the Quran has remained unchanged and will remain so as Allah Himself has guaranteed its preservation and protection.

** The Quran is only available in Arabic, its original language. The Quran’s beauty, style, miraculous nature and its message can only be understood in full extent by understanding Arabic. But for the benefit of the non-Arabs the meaning of the Quran has been translated in many languages.**

**SOME IMPORTANT FACTS ABOUT THE QURAN**
**************************************************************
* The Quran has 114 chapters (Surahs) which have more than 6000 verses. Surahs within Quran are not organized in the chronological sequence of their revelation. In some cases verses within a surah are not organized in the chronological order of their revelation. The order of the verses within a surah and the order of the surahs within the Quran were revealed from Allah.
* The word Quran means reading or recitation indicating the fact that the Quran is to be both recited from memory and read from text. All Muslims have to memorize some parts of the Quran and there are many who have memorized the entire Quran.
* The Quran’s fundamental message is to submit to Allah and worship Him alone. As a complete guide, it covers all aspects of life. Examples of some topics in the Quran include the following but are not limited to: defining the creed; expounding moral instruction; admonishing believers and non- believers; inviting people to explore, question and validate the truth it proclaims; giving good news of afterlife; laying down laws from dealing with mundane matters of daily lives to matters of warfare; drawing lessons from historical events etc.
* Verses (and Surahs) revealed before Prophet Muhammad's (pbuh) migration known as Hijra to Madina are known as Makki, and those revealed after migration (Hijra) to Madina are known as Madani (with few exceptions).
* The Quran is a unique book. Its theme, style, order, and arrangement of information is unlike any other book. Readers are likely to notice that diverse kinds of materials exist side by side. It may seem confusing to a new reader as it will navigate through different topics and at times may seem to change from on topic to another without any notice or indication.
* The Quran does not relate history in a conventional sense. The Quran narrates many narratives in brief and at times in details including stories of previous Prophets. Whenever the Quran brings up a snap shot of history, it is for a reason. That reason may be correcting what was related in other religious books, or to teach a lesson, or show an example.
* The Quran is not a book of science but it relates signs for the curious and hungry mind to contemplate. One may find many signs indicating well established scientific facts like embryology, origin of universe, earth's shape, earth and suns rotation, expansion of the universe, every living being made of water and many others. No established scientific fact is ever in disagreement with the signs related in the Quran.
* In the Quran, God at times refers to Himself in 1st person, third person (he) and at times 1st person plural (we or us). In Arabic, it is as correct to use the plural when speaking of one person as it is to use the singular. But the plural is used for respect and glorification, and no one is more deserving of respect and glorification than God. So the singular is used to affirm the fact that He is One and has no partner or associate, and the plural is used to affirm His glory and majesty, may He be exalted.
* The Quran at times gives details about a topic and at times gives little details about a topic. At times, the background and circumstances under which particular verses containing orders or rules were revealed have not been mentioned. But keep in mind God didn’t just send His final revelation but He sent along with it the final Messenger Prophet Muhammad, who had lived according to that complete guidance and gave us the details of the complete guidance by practice and instruction.

Article # 5: HADITH (THE SECONDARY SOURCE OF ISLAMIC INFORMATION)
** Hadith (or Sunnah) is the collection of commands, sayings, actions, expressed approvals/disapprovals and implicit approvals of Prophet Muhammad. It explains many aspects of the Quran and help us understand it. Hadith were also memorized and recorded meticulously by the Prophet’s companions and passed on to successive generations. There are eight widely used Hadith Collections and of them Six are most famous.

**Every one of us should have some basic knowledge of hadith, the famous collections and classification (based on authenticity) of hadith . Familiarize yourself with the hadith collections of Imam Bukhari and Muslim.

**(1) Sahih Al-Bukhari: Generally regarded as the single most authentic collection of hadith. Sahih Al-Bukhari covers topics from almost all aspects of life in providing proper guidance from the messenger of Allah. In Imam Bukhari's 'Al-Jami-al-Sahih' (Sahih Al Bukhari) the Imam (born within 180 years of Prophet's death) had recorded all the hadiths of the Prophet which he found to be genuine after thorough examination and scrutiny. He spent sixteen years in research and examined more than sixty thousand hadiths from which he selected only hadiths whose genuineness and accuracy is established beyond the slightest doubt. Deducting duplicates, the Imam's collection contain about four thousand distinct hadiths.
*Access online from https://www.sunnah.com/bukhari.

**(2) Sahih Muslim: Sahih Muslim is the most authentic book of Hadith after Sahih Al-Bukhari and contains about 7,500 hadiths. Imam Muslim (born within 190 years of Prophet's death) is one of the foremost preservers of hadith of the Prophet. His book comes second only to Sahih al Bukhari in terms of authenticity according to majority of scholars.
*Access online from www.sunnah.com.

**The other four prominent Hadith collections are:
These four collections contains mostly authentic but also some weak (doubtful or deficient in proof of authenticity) hadiths. Therefore these must be approached with careful consideration. In some of the recent versions of these collections, hadiths are marked with the grade of authenticity for our benefit. The two other widely used ones are "Muwatta Imam Malik" and "Musnad Ahmad ibn Hanbal".

Article # 7: REQUIRED DAILY PRAYER (SALAH)
**The Prayer (known as "Salah" in Arabic or Namaz in some other languages) is the most important and greatest act of worship in Islam.

* By the order of Allah a Muslim is required to offer Prayer (Salah) five times a day.

* The five daily Prayers must be offered at specified time which are at dawn, noon, mid-afternoon, sunset and nightfall.

* Allah commands us to observe the required five daily prayers (Salah) under all circumstances, even when we are sick or travelling.

* Prayer (Salah) establishes a direct personal and spiritual connection between us and Allah, and is a constant and practical reminder of a Muslim's duty to surrender to Allah.

* The Prayers (Salah) develop in a Muslim the qualities of self-discipline, steadfastness and obedience to God, leading one to be patient, honest and truthful in the affairs of his/her life.

Article # 8: REQUIRED YEARLY CHARITY (ZAKAH):
REQUIRED YEARLY CHARITY (ZAKAH):


** "Obligatory Charity" (known as "Zakah / Zakat" in Arabic) is an important act of worship in Islam.

*The word Zakah means 'to cleanse' or 'to purify'. And Islamically the act of giving zakah purifies an individual's wealth and himself.

*Zakah is an obligatory charity due on wealth, above certain levels, and to be distributed to specific groups of people at specific time.

*Zakah is a pillar of Islam and it is very important as it is mentioned and linked to Salah in more than 80 places in Quran.

*Zakah is to be calculated once(same time) a year after a muslim become owner of the excess wealth for one year.

*A Muslim who doesn't have excess wealth beyond the prescribed amount does not have to pay Zakah.

*Example of excess wealth which are part of Zakah calculation, includes (1)gold, (2)silver,(3) money, (4)some agricultural produce, (5)business inventory and (6)some livestock, etc.

*In most cases Zakah is 2.5% of the excess wealth.

*Zakah is given primarily to poor and needy Muslims. Zakah money can be spent on disaster relief, free slaves and captives of war. Zakah money can also be spent on certain debt relief; traveler in need; and struggling and striving in the cause of Allah.

*Zakah cannot be given as donations to mosques (masjids) or religious institutions.

*By giving Zakah not only we purify our wealth but also help in the process of building a good society. Zakah ensures flow of wealth from rich to poor. It reduces the gap between the rich and the poor and ensures an equitable society where everyone has a right to contribute and share.

*There will be severe punishment for those how do not pay Zakah which is mentioned in many places in Quran and Hadith.

Article # 11: RULES OF DRESS/CLOTHING (DRESS CODE)
Islam is a complete way of life, hence Guidance of Allah also contains general (or specific on certain things) directions for each and every aspect of our life. And from that perspective our Shariah (Islamic law) gave us general directions on how to dress modestly and while prohibits certain things.

Brief description of general guidelines and some prohibitions
***********************************************************************
*Both men and women are expected to dress in a way which is modest and dignified.
*No particular types or styles of dress is recommended.
*Men must cover their body modestly and the minimum amount to be covered is between the navel and the knee. Women must cover their entire body except the face and hands when in public or in the presence of 'non-Mahram' men.
*Clothing should be loose fitting to the point that it may not reveal the shape of the body (which is required to be covered).
*Dressing (or clothing) imitating opposite gender (men dressing like women or vice versa) is not allowed.
*Dressing (or clothing) imitating religious personality ( such as Roman Catholic priest or monks) of other religion or using religious symbols (such as cross) of other religion is not allowed.
*Clothing made of silk and dressing with gold is not allowed for men.

Mahram is a person, man or woman related to a particular individual by blood, marriage or breastfeeding. Mahram is a person, we are not permitted to marry and do not have to observe strict dress restrictions.
*Mahram due to close blood relationship: For a woman, her father, grandfather, son, grandson, brother, both paternal and maternal uncles and nephews. Likewise for a man, his mother, daughter, granddaughter, sister, both paternal and maternal aunties and nieces.
Mahram due to Breastfeeding: This includes anyone, man or woman breastfed by the same mother or wet nurse. Also includes the brother or husband of the wet nurse.
Mahram due to Marriage: People who become our relatives by marriage, such as father-in-law, mother-in-law, stepfather, stepmother, stepson, stepdaughter.

Awrah is the parts of the body that should be kept covered. A man's Awrah is from navel to knee and a women's Awrah is her entire body except face and hands.
There is no Awrah between a husband and wife.
When a woman is amongst women or her Mahrams, she does not have to observe strict rules of covering. And she is able to uncover her hair, face, neck, arms, hands, legs from below the knee, and feet. A desired trait of a Muslim woman is Haya (shyness and modesty), therefore she must never make a wanton display of herself.

Hijab
***********
The word Hijab means conceal, hide or screen. It is popularly known to be a woman’s headscarf (a veil which usually covers the head and chest) but actually hijab is much more than that. It is a broader term meaning modest clothing and behavior. In terms of clothing it would mean using the proper dress to cover the Awrah.
** Women must cover their entire body except the face and hands for prayer (Salah) whether in public or private.
** For men it is recommended to cover from shoulder to below the knee while must cover Awrah at all times during prayer (salah) whether in public or private.

It is permissible for women (not for men) to use cosmetics for beautification as long as it meets the following four conditions.
*(1) This beautification should not be for non-Mahram men.
*(2) The materials used for cosmetic purposes should be of permissible (such as Henna, kohl, lipstick, blusher, nail polish, etc) substances and do not contain or made from impure (such as pig fat) substances.
*(3) The materials used for cosmetic purposes should not be harmful to her body.
*(4) The cosmetic effect on the body should be temporary. It is not permissible to use substances that change the creation of Allah, such as lip treatments, tattoos, etc.
** Cosmetics that form layers such as nail polish must be removed before ablution (Wudoo).

Plucking eyebrows for beautification is forbidden in Islam.

Article # 12: DIETARY LAWS IN ISLAM (RULES OF FOOD AND DRINK)
** In Islam every food and drink is permissible unless it is prohibited by Allah or His Messenger (pbuh).

** As Muslims we believe the ultimate purpose of food consumption is to maintain a healthy body which will enable us to worship Allah.**

Brief description of general guidelines and some prohibitions:

** A key point to remember is that we eat to live, and not live to eat.**

** Halal (permissible) food means food (or drink) that are allowed for us it consume.
** Haram (Forbidden or prohibited) food means food (or drink) that are not allowed for us to consume.

** Any food (or drink) that is harmful for health or life is haram.
All kind of grains, vegetables, beans, lentils and fruits are permissible, and nothing has been explicitly forbidden in Islam.
** All kind of sea plants and fishes (seafood, that can only survive in water) are permissible.

** Blood consumption is forbidden.**

** Meat from Chicken, duck, turkey, cow, goat, lamb, camel, certain kind of birds, etc. are permissible as long as they are slaughtered according to Islamic law. Meat of the these dead animals (if they die before slaughtering) are prohibited.**

** Pigs, all meat eating wild animals (tigers, cats, dogs, etc), all predatory birds (eagles, falcons, etc.), all land insects (except locusts), snakes, mouse, etc. are forbidden for consumption.**

*** Alcohol and drugs are forbidden.

**Etiquette of Eating and Drinking:
* Avoid of eating or drinking in gold, silver, gold-plated or silver plated dishes, glasses or utensils.
* Wash hands before eating.
* Always remember and start with the name of Allah saying "Bismillah".
* Eat with right hand or using right hand.
* Avoid excessive eating. It is recommended to fill one third of stomach with food, one third with water and leave one third for air.
* It is ok, to not like certain kind of food but we should not criticize any food.

** Finish eating expressing thank and praise to Allah saying, "Al-hamdu lillaah" (All praise and thank is due to Allah).

Do not waste food and do not be extravagant.

Article # 13: RULES OF FAMILY AND MARRIAGE
** Family is the central entity of Islamic society. A Muslim is always part of a family as a child, parent or grandparent. Living alone and single life is discouraged in Islam.

** Allah has created men and women as company for one another (as husband and wife) and to procreate (for those of us able to have children) and live in peace and tranquility according to the Guidance of Allah.

** Unlike other religions (like Christianity, Buddhism, Judaism etc.) which consider celibacy or monasticism as a great virtue, Islam considers marriage and family life as one of the greatest virtues.

** All forms of free mixing and relationship between a men and women outside of a marriage is prohibited.

** Marriage is only between a Muslim man and a Muslim woman. In certain circumstances marriage between a Muslim man and a woman from the people of the book (Jews, Christians) may be allowed but are strongly discouraged. A new Muslim woman who is already married to a non Muslim husband should contact a local scholar to discuss her particular situation.

** The husband and wife are complimentary partners of the family who are not identical but are equal in the sight of Allah and play their respective role in the family and society.

** Within a family a man (in the role of a husband and father) is primarily responsible for (1) Leadership, (2) Financial/Economic security (provider of food, lodging and all basic necessities of life) (3) Guidance and education (4) Protection and safety.

** Within a family a woman (in the role of a wife and mother) is primarily responsible for (1) Housekeeping and cooking (2) Nurture, care and comfort (3) Advice and education (4) Keep-up with family ties and Preserve the honor and dignity of the family.

** Marriage is meant to last a lifetime (and beyond). There is no concept of temporary marriage in Islam.

** Divorce (Talaq) is allowed but are very very strongly discouraged and disliked.

** Marriage and Divorce are valid only when those are performed according to Islamic law under the authority of local Muslim Imam (Scholar of the mosque) with proper documentation. New Muslims are strongly advised to be careful, and learn the requirements and responsibilities of marriage before making such a decision.**

** It is an obligation upon every Muslim to take care of their elderly parents (even if they are non-Muslims) and grandparents. While the financial obligation (food, lodging and basic necessities) is primarily upon the son but the rest of the responsibilities may be shared between son and daughter.

Article # 14: RULES OF EMPLOYMENT AND FINANCIAL TRANSACTION
** Earning a Halal (permissible/lawful) living whether through business or employment is a requirement in Islam. Allah has commanded us to work to earn our living and encouraged us not to depend on others.**

** Islam laid down certain guidelines for business and employment for the creation of an economy based on fair play and justice. The guidelines takes into account people's (both seller and buyer) interest, protects professional and financial rights, and resolve conflicts.

## Brief description of general guidelines and some prohibitions:

* All kind of employment and business is permissible (Halal) unless our Shariah prohibits it. So, it is important to know what kind of employment and business dealings are prohibited (Haram).

* Employment or business that deals with prohibited (Haram) items (products or concepts), such as usury/interest (Riba), gambling, lottery, prostitution, pornography, Alcohol, pork, etc. are prohibited (Haram). So, for example selling, distributing or working in any shape or form that involves alcohol (such as a Bar or restaurant) is prohibited (Haram).**

* Employment that are prohibited (Haram) due to certain conditions of the employment. So, for example if the employment requires an employee to shave his beard or dress in impermissible clothing or do not allow to fast/pray, etc. then that employment becomes prohibited (Haram).**

* Conditions that make a business prohibited (Haram). That means in business, selling permissible product, with deceptive means or techniques or inadequate information about quantity and quality or selling uncertainty (hidden amount or items or price) are prohibited (Haram).**

* In any transaction (employment or business) it is prohibited to make an offer when someone is already negotiating a deal. For example, if someone is negotiating a price of a car with the dealer for $2000 dollars, it is prohibited to interfere with an offer of $2500. One must wait until that deal negotiating ends or if the person (the first customer) allows him to do so.**

* All forms of bribery (giving or taking) and corruption in workplace and business is prohibited in Islam.**

** Finally, Islam puts income and financial obligation upon the muslim man (as husband or father). It is not an obligation put upon woman.**

Article # 15: RULES OF MORAL CHARACTER

** All human beings are created with certain natural tendencies of understanding good and evil. Islam clearly identifies good and evil; what is acceptable and what is unacceptable; what is moral and what is immoral; and thus leaves no ambiguity for us humans to set new standards.**

Article # 16: A LIST OF COMMON ISLAMIC PHRASES AND EXPRESSIONS
This is a list of Arabic words or Phrases used by all Muslims irrespective of their language. Meaning and appropriate usage is mentioned.

(1) When You Meet a Muslim
*********************************
(a) Greet with saying "As-Salaamu-alaikum" ( which means "peace be upon you").
Respond with saying "Wa-alaikumus-Salam" (and peace be with you).
(b) or say "As-Salaamu-alaikum wa Rahmatullah" (Peace and mercy of Allah be upon you)
Respond with "Wa-alaikumus-Salam wa Rahmatullah" (and Peace and mercy of Allah be upon you)
(c) or say "As-Salaamu-alaikum wa Rahmatullahi wa Barakatuhu" (…… and His blessings)
Respond with "Wa-alaikumus-Salam wa Rahmatullahi wa Barakatuhu " (……… and His blessings).

(2) When start any (with few exceptions) Halal speech or action, Say
*****************************************************************************
(a) Bismillah (in the name of Allah).
(b) or, "Bismillahir-Rahmaan- ir-Raheem" (…the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful)

(3) When you want to Thank and or show Gratitude to Allah, Say
**************************************************************************
Alhamdulillah- (all thank and praise is for Allah). An acknowledgement of content for everything we have (or given) and whatever conditions we are kept in by Allah. This phrase is known as Tahmid.

(4) When Intend To Do Something, Say
********************************************
In Sha Allah (Allah willing or if Allah wills). A reminder and acknowledgment that nothing can happen except by the will of Allah.

(5) When you’re surprised or amazed by beauty or good, Say
**********************************************************************
SubhanAllah (Glory be to Allah). An acknowledgement that Allah is perfect, above anything in the creation, and everything that comes from Him is perfect. This phrase in known as Tasbih.

(6) When admiring or praising someone, Say
****************************************************
MashaAllah (as Allah wills or what Allah wills). An acknowledgement that everything comes from Allah.

(7) When Thanking Someone
**********************************
(a) Say, "Jazak-Allahu Khair" (may Allah reward you with good).
Respond with ""Wa iy-yakum" (and to you) or "Wa Antum, fa-Jazak-Allahu Khair" (and you too…..)
(b) or Say, "Barak-Allah feek" (may Allah bless you).
Respond with "Wa iy-yakum" (and to you).

(8) When you think you saw or did something wrong ( sin, mistake), Say
*********************************************************************************
Astaghfirullah (I seek the forgiveness of Allah). Usually uttered in silence.

(9) When approve of what you hear, see, to praise a speaker or express excitement, Say
*********************************************************************************
Allahu Akbar (Allah is greater or Allah is the greatest). This phrase is known as Takbir.

(10) When scared or feeling unsafe from evil thoughts or places, Say
*****************************************************************************
(a) Audhu billah (I seek refuge in and with Allah).
(b) or, "Audhu billahi minash-Shaytanir-Rajeem" (…. from Shaitan, the outcust)

(11) When struck with calamity, or taken over by a situation beyond your control, Say
***************************************************************************************
La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah (There is no power nor strength expcept by Allah)

(12) When afflicted by a calamity, misfortune or upon hearing news of death, Say
***************************************************************************************
Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi rajiun (Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return)

(13) When you Sneeze, Say
*******************************
Alhamdulillah- (all thank and praise is for Allah).

(14) When someone Sneezes and says, "Alhamdulillah", Say
*********************************************************************
Yarhamuka Allah (May Allah have mercy upon you)

(15) When you spend time, money or effort only for Allah's Pleasure, Say
**********************************************************************************
Fi Sabilillah ('In the way of Allah' or 'For the cause of Allah' or 'for the sake of Allah'). You may use this term to let someone know that you do not want any worldly benefit in return, but not to show-off your generosity.

(16) When you mention Prophet Muhammad's (saws) name or hear being mentioned, Say
**************************************************************************************
Sallahu alayhi wa Sallam (May Allah's peace and blessing be upon him). Written abbreviation (SAWS, SAW, SAS or pbuh).

(17) When you mention any prophet's name or hear being mentioned, Say
************************************************************************************
Alayhis Salam (May Allah's Peace be upon him). Written abbreviation (AS or pbuh)

(18) When parting with someone, Say
********************************************
Fi Amanallah (In the Protection of Allah). Allah Hafiz (Allah is the Protector) is also used.

(19) When you end an answer to a Religious Question, Say
*******************************************************************
Allahu Alam (Allah knows the best).

(20) Following are optional but widely used phrases
***********************************************************
(a) RadiAllahu anhu (May Allah be pleased with him). Used after names of Companions of the Prophet (pbuh). Written abbreviation (RA).

(b) Rahimahullah (may Allah have mercy on him). Used after names of well known scholars of the past. Another variation "Rahmatullah Alaihi" of same meaning is also in use.
